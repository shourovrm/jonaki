Jonaki — cost and model switching

Three ways to show what a thread costs and to switch between your chosen models inside a thread. Both use the Firefly look and the step track. Prices and costs are sample numbers. Your favourite models (here called "scoped models", as in pi) are picked once in Settings.

A · Inline

Cost
The thread's running total sits in the subtitle under the title. Each run's line adds its own cost next to steps and time. The thread list shows the total under the time.
Models
A model chip at the left of the message field. Tapping it opens a sheet with your scoped models and their prices; the choice holds for this thread from the next message.
Strength
Cost and model are visible where you type, so you see them before you send. One tap to switch.
Risk
The composer gets busier, and a long model name has to be shortened in the chip.

B · On demand

Cost
A small cost chip in the app bar. Tapping it opens the thread's usage: total, tokens in, out and cached, and a line per model used.
Models
Tapping the subtitle under the title opens a menu of your scoped models. The composer stays as it is now.
Strength
The chat stays clean, and the usage sheet explains where the money went, including cache savings.
Risk
Switching models is less discoverable: the subtitle doesn't look like a button.

C · Status strip

What
One thin row of icons with numbers: the model (tap to switch), the context window size, how much of it is used, and the cost so far in this thread. It sits either under the title or just above the message field.
Help
Settings has a "Status icons" page that names each icon in one line, with switches for showing the strip and where.
Strength
Everything that changes while you chat is in one place and always visible, like a code editor's status bar. Model switching and cost need no extra screen.
Risk
Icons need learning once. At the bottom it moves with the keyboard; at the top it costs one row of chat.

All · Scoped models in Settings

What
One list of the models you want to switch between. Add from any provider you have a key for; OpenRouter models come with their prices. The first one is the default for new threads.
Note
Prices come from OpenRouter's model list; for DeepSeek and Gemini they are a small built-in table that you can edit.
